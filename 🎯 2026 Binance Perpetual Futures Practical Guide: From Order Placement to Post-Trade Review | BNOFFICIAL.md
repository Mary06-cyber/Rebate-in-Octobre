This guide assumes you have already opened Binance Futures and are preparing to place a BTCUSDT perpetual contract trade. The first step is NOT clicking Buy/Long or Sell/Short. Instead, break every trade into five phases: Planning, Execution, Management, Exit, and Review.

## 📌 Phase 1: Build a Trading Plan

Before placing any order, answer these questions: Long or Short? Why? Where to enter? Where to exit if wrong? Where to take profit if right? What is the maximum loss?

Do not just write bullish or bearish. A complete plan requires: Direction, Entry Zone, Invalidation, Stop Loss, Target, and Maximum Loss.

Entry Zone is more flexible than a single Entry price. Markets rarely hit exactly one number, so define a price area where you are willing to open a position.

Invalidation tells you in advance what conditions would prove your thesis wrong. This makes exiting rational rather than emotional.

Stop Loss should be tied to your trade logic. If price breaks a certain level, your original logic no longer holds. That level is where your stop belongs, not just a random line because you feel scared of losing.

Maximum Loss determines your Position Size. For example, if you are willing to lose max 20 USDT on this trade, you can calculate Position Size by combining your Stop Loss distance and Maximum Loss.

Never use the 25%, 50%, 75%, or 100% buttons directly. These presets tell you nothing about whether your risk is reasonable. What matters is the maximum loss after combining Position Size with Stop Loss distance.

## 🪪 Phase 2: Choose Margin Mode

Isolated Margin: Each position uses independent margin. Liquidation only loses the margin allocated to that specific position. This is ideal for single-trade risk control and recommended for beginners.

Cross Margin: The entire futures wallet balance supports the position. It can withstand small wicks better but carries the risk of one losing trade dragging down the whole account.

## ⚙️ Phase 3: Set Leverage Last

Leverage is the final parameter. Only after Position Size, Maximum Loss, and Stop Loss are clear does Leverage become meaningful.

Never treat Leverage as a profit multiplier button. It simultaneously affects Margin usage, Liquidation Distance, position structure, and how fast your risk evaporates. Higher leverage does not mean a better trade.

## 📦 Choose Order Type

The two fundamentals: Market and Limit.

Market Order: Executes immediately at available prices. It is Taker order logic, suitable when current price matches your plan, but you must accept potential Slippage.

Limit Order: Waits for your specified price. If price never reaches, no fill occurs. This enforces discipline by itself.

Do not let FOMO destroy your Limit plan. Originally placed a Limit, but price moved away, so you cancel and chase with Market. At that point, you are no longer executing your original plan.

Pre-order checklist: Correct trading pair? Correct Long/Short? Correct Isolated/Cross? Correct Position Size? Correct Leverage? Correct Stop Loss? Correct Take Profit?

## 🛡 Phase 4: Manage the Position After Fill

Once filled, monitor: Entry Price, Mark Price, Position Size, Margin, Liquidation Price, and PnL.

Entry Price is your actual average cost. A Market Order may fill across multiple price levels, so the price you saw before clicking and your Average Entry Price may differ.

Mark Price is the risk reference. It is used in PnL, Risk, and Liquidation calculations. It is not simply the latest trade price.

Do not just stare at red and green PnL numbers. Constantly watching +50, -30, +80, -100 leads to emotional decisions. Focus on whether the trade structure is still intact.

Stop Loss must not be moved outward just because of floating losses. The common trap: price approaches Stop, you move it further, lose more, move it again, until your original Risk Plan is completely broken.

Canceling a Stop Loss changes the entire risk structure. If your Position Size was calculated based on a specific Stop, removing it means your maximum loss no longer exists on paper.

Take Profit should not be endlessly moved outward either. Reaching planned TP but feeling greedy, moving it further, then watching price reverse, is another form of plan failure.

Design Partial Take Profit in advance: TP1 close 30%, TP2 close 30%, remaining 40% continue managing. This converts some floating profit into Realized PnL.

Reduce Only is perfect for closing orders. It ensures the order only reduces an existing position, minimizing the risk of accidentally opening a reverse position.

Do not ignore Funding times. Perpetual Futures have Funding Rates. Holding through Funding time may incur Funding Fees, which could be paid or received depending on Funding Rate and your Position Direction.

The longer you hold, the more costs matter. Short-term trades focus on Entry/Exit fees. Long-term holds must also factor in Funding, Spread, Slippage, and other product costs.

## 🔍 Phase 5: Do Not Open the Next Trade Immediately After Closing

After closing, ask: What did I do well? What went wrong? Did I follow the plan?

Record 10 fields: Date, Pair, Direction, Entry, Stop, Target, Position Size, Leverage, Result, Mistake.

Review is not about whether you made or lost money. A losing trade can be perfectly executed while the market simply did not cooperate. A winning trade can be pure luck from undisciplined actions. The review focus should be: Did I follow my plan?

## ⚠️ 6 Actions That Most Often Destroy Trading Plans

Chasing Market after Limit fails to fill, moving Stop outward on floating losses, averaging down with consecutive adds, suddenly increasing Position Size, canceling Take Profit after going profitable, revenge trading immediately after closing a losing position.

Larger Position Size does not mean larger opportunity. Increasing size increases both potential profit and potential loss. Position sizing must not revolve solely around how much you want to make.

What contracts truly need to control is Drawdown. More important than single-trade profit are: maximum account drawdown, consecutive losses, single-trade risk, and total risk exposure.
