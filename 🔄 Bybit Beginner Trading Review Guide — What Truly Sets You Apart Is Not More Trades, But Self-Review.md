## 1. What Is Trade Review?

Simply: After a trade ends, re-check why you entered, why you exited, and what went wrong.

Review is not just about: "Did this trade make or lose money?"

It is about: "Did this trade follow the original plan?"

Because:

A profitable trade can still be a wrong operation.
A losing trade can still be correct execution.

## 2. Profit Does Not Always Mean the Trade Was Right

Example:

You had no plan. BTC suddenly pumped, so you chased a Long. Price continued up. You made money.

Many think: "I have great judgment."

But logically, this was likely: a trade without a plan that happened to work out.

Repeat this enough times, and a big reversal will eventually hit.

## 3. A Loss Does Not Always Mean You Were Wrong

Another scenario:

You preset: Entry, Stop Loss, Take Profit, max risk.
Market moved against you, hit Stop Loss. Small loss.

But throughout: no over-adding, no moving SL, no revenge trading, loss within expectation.

This trade lost money, but execution may have been correct.

## 4. First Review Question: Why Did You Enter?

After every trade, ask: "Why did I enter?"

Record: breakout, pullback, support, resistance, market structure, planned price, other clear reasons.

If the reason was just "felt like it would go up" or "everyone else is buying," that's what needs improvement.

## 5. Second Question: Did You Enter Too Early?

Many trades have the right direction but poor timing.

Example: planned to wait for BTC pullback. Price started moving up, FOMO hit, entered immediately.

Even if direction was right, poor entry cost and wide SL hurt the trade quality.

Ask: "If I could redo this, would I wait for a better entry?"

## 6. Third Question: Was Position Size Larger Than Planned?

Planned small, but got excited and sized up.

Record: planned size vs actual size. Big gap = emotional influence.

## 7. Fourth Question: Did You Move Your Stop Loss?

Critical check.

Original SL at A. Price approaching A, you thought "give it more room," moved to B. Then C. Small loss became big loss.

Record: Did you violate original SL rules?

## 8. Fifth Question: Did You Exit Profits Too Early?

Some people don't know how to lose, others fear losing profits.

Example: TP set higher, small floating profit appeared, closed everything immediately. Market later hit original target.

Repeating this creates: small wins, normal losses. Long-term structure deteriorates.

## 9. Sixth Question: Did You Hold Too Long?

Opposite extreme: clear profit, but kept thinking "maybe more."

+100 became +70, then +30, then back to 0.

Record: Did you follow original target? Reasons: greed, changed mind, no target, wanting more.

## 10. Seventh Question: Was This an Emotional Trade?

Two types:

Planned trade: follows rules.
Emotional trade: caused by FOMO, fear of missing out, revenge trading, boredom, wanting to recover losses, overconfidence.

If 6 of 10 weekly trades are emotional, the problem is not analysis — it is unplanned trading.

## 13. Do Not Only Review Losing Trades

Many only review losses. Profitable trades need review too.

Wrong profits build wrong habits. One lucky high-leverage trade does not validate the method.

## 14. Each Week, Find the "Most Frequent Error"

Don't fix everything at once.

This week 6 of 10 trades had "entered too early" → next week only practice waiting.

Stop-loss moving → next week only practice not touching SL.

One fix at a time.

## 15. Score Your Trades

After each trade, score 0–5:

5: Fully planned.
4: Minor issues, overall good.
3: Clear impulsive decision.
2: Violated size or SL rules.
1: Mostly emotional.
0: No plan at all.

Score by rule execution, not P&L.

After 20–30 trades, look for the most repeated error: chasing, bottom fishing, high leverage, no SL, early TP, overtrading, revenge trading.

Find the #1 repeat offender = your top improvement target.
