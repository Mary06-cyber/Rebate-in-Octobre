## 3️⃣ If You Only Want to Buy Coins, Start with Spot

Spot is the easiest to understand: use USDT to buy BTC, and after execution your account actually holds BTC.

Example:
You have 500 USDT and want to use 100 USDT to buy BTC. Open BTCUSDT, choose buy side, submit a market or limit order. After fill, holdings show BTC quantity, and later you can sell back to USDT.

Spot notes:
- Ordinary non-leveraged spot does not rely on forced liquidation caused by insufficient margin.
- Falling coin prices still create unrealized or realized losses.
- It is the best place to learn order placement, position viewing and trade history.

## 4️⃣ How to Choose Market and Limit Orders

Market order: use when you want immediate execution. Simple and fast, but during volatile moves slippage may occur and the filled price may differ from the quoted price.

Limit order: use when you want execution only at a specified price or better. For example, if BTC current price is above your intended buy level, pre-place a lower buy limit. It will not fill until price reaches it. Advantage is price control; disadvantage is missing the move if price never arrives.

New users should try one small market order and one small limit order to feel the difference.

## 5️⃣ First Coin Purchase Does Not Need to Be All at Once

With 1,000 USDT, consider splitting:
- 250 USDT for first base position.
- 250 USDT to add on pullback or confirmation.
- 250 USDT reserved for further adjustment.
- 250 USDT kept as standby cash.

Staggered buying reduces the pressure of chasing highs once. If everything is deployed in one order, a reversal leaves almost no room to adjust.

## 7️⃣ When to Start Studying Futures

Learn these basics first:
- Buy and sell spot.
- View positions and trade history.
- Use limit and market orders.
- Set stops and control single-trade size.

Futures are not simply an extra leverage button. They add long, short, margin, liquidation price, funding rate and position mode. Without solid basics, high leverage leaves very little error margin.

## 8️⃣ What Are Long and Short

Long: if you expect price to rise, study long positions. Rising price benefits longs; falling price causes losses.
Short: if you expect price to fall, study short positions. Falling price benefits shorts; rising price causes losses.

Either side can lose due to wrong direction. A short is not forbidden just because the market has a long-term upward bias; what matters is plan, stop loss and size.

## 9️⃣ Leverage Risk Matters More Than Multiples

Many only look at 10x, 20x, 50x, but actual position is more important. With the same 10x:
- Small size with tight stop and far liquidation level is manageable.
- Large size with wide stop and near liquidation level can fail with one adverse move.

Before opening, check together:
- Leverage multiple.
- Notional position value.
- Available margin.
- Estimated liquidation price.
- Stop loss price.
- Maximum acceptable loss per trade.
Use fixed risk to back-calculate size instead of simply picking high leverage.

## 🔟 Isolated and Cross Margin Must Be Chosen Correctly

Isolated margin: a single position uses independent margin. Maximum loss is easier to bound within that position, good for understanding per-trade risk.

Cross margin: more available account funds may support the same position, so liquidation price may be farther, but extreme moves can expose more funds. Cross is not automatically safer; it only distributes risk differently.

Before choosing, decide the maximum loss you accept and whether you want it confined to one isolated bucket.

## 1️⃣1️⃣ Exit Matters More Than Entry

Many spend much time finding where to enter but little time planning how to exit if wrong. Before every trade answer at least:
- Where is entry.
- Where is stop loss.
- Where is take-profit or first reduction.
- If price never triggers entry, will you skip the trade.

Without an exit rule, the trade is incomplete. Stop loss is part of planning, not a repair after losses occur.
