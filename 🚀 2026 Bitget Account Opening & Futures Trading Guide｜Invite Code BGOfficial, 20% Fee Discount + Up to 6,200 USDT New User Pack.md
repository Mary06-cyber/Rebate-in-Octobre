## 1️⃣ Set a "Risk Budget" Before Futures Trading
Assume your account has 1,000 USDT. Don't start by thinking "How much should I open?" Instead ask: "If I'm wrong, what's the maximum I can accept to lose?" (e.g., 10 USDT). Stop-loss distance, position size, and leverage should all be designed around this **Risk Budget**.

## 2️⃣ Find the "Invalidation Point" Before Entry
Don't just stare at the chart. A better question: "Where would prove my trading logic wrong?" For example, if you're long on BTC and price clearly breaks below a key support, that logic is invalidated — this level becomes your primary stop-loss reference.

## 3️⃣ Two Basic Directions on Bitget Futures
- **Long**: Expecting price to rise. Valid conditions include support holding, breakout retest, or strength in market structure.
- **Short**: Expecting price to fall. Valid conditions include resistance rejection, key support breakdown, or weakening bounce.

## 4️⃣ Decide Leverage Last
Correct order: Max Loss → Stop Distance → Position Size → Leverage. High leverage shrinks error margin and brings liquidation closer. **High leverage + large position** is the #1 cause of liquidation for beginners.

## 5️⃣ Isolated vs Cross Margin
- **Isolated**: Independent margin per position; risk is easy to cap and visualize.
- **Cross**: Uses more available balance; liquidation price may seem farther, but more funds are exposed. Farther liquidation ≠ safer.

## 6️⃣ Write Down These 5 Prices Before Entry
1. **Entry** (entry price)
2. **Stop Loss** (where logic fails)
3. **TP1** (first take-profit)
4. **TP2** (second/extension take-profit)
5. **Liquidation Price** (forced close price)
Do NOT open a position if you don't have clear answers for all five.
