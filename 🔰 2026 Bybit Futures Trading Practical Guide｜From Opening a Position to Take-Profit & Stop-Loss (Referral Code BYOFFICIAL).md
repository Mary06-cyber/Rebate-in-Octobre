## 🟢 4. What's the Difference Between Isolated and Cross Margin?

| Mode | Logic | Pros | Cons | Best For |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Isolated | Each position's margin is independent | Risk isolation; max loss per trade is clear | Liquidation price may be closer | Beginners, those wanting single-trade risk control |
| Cross | Account's available balance supports position together | Liquidation price may be further away | More funds can be involved | Experienced traders with mature risk management |

Isolated margin suits beginners because the risk ceiling of each trade is clearer. Cross margin is NOT simply "safer" — it draws more account funds to support the position.

## 🟢 5. Higher Leverage Is NOT Better

| Leverage | Margin Required | Distance to Liquidation | Margin for Error | Suitable For |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1x–5x | Higher | Further | Large | Beginners, conservative traders |
| 10x–20x | Medium | Medium | Medium | Experienced traders |
| 50x–100x | Very low | Very close | Very small | Advanced traders, high risk tolerance |

Leverage amplifies both risk and profit. High leverage doesn't mean higher skill — it just shrinks your margin for error. Beginners should use small positions + low leverage, NOT small capital + ultra-high leverage.

## 🟢 8. Market Order vs Limit Order?

| Order Type | Best For | Pros | Cons |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Market Order | Quick execution, chasing breakouts | Simple, fast fill | Slippage during high volatility |
| Limit Order | Control entry price, wait for pullbacks | Price control, good for support/resistance | Won't fill if price doesn't reach |

Beginners are advised to practice with Limit orders first, as they let you set entry price in advance and avoid emotional chasing.

## 🟢 9. Why Plan Take-Profit & Stop-Loss Before Opening?

| Type | Function | Common Mistake |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Stop-Loss | Exit early when price goes wrong; prevent small mistakes from becoming big losses | Holding losers hoping for recovery |
| Take-Profit | Close at planned target when price reaches it | Refusing to close winners, letting profits fade |

The correct sequence: define exit levels first, then open. If you don't know where you'd admit being wrong, you shouldn't open at all.

## 🟢 10. What Is Liquidation?

| Concept | Explanation |
| :--- | :--- |
| Forced Liquidation | System automatically closes position when margin is insufficient |
| Trigger | Market moves adversely; margin falls below maintenance requirement |
| Prevention | Control leverage, control position size, set stop-loss, keep sufficient margin |
| Must Check Before Opening | Entry price, liquidation price, stop-loss price, leverage, position size, available margin |

Don't wait until you're near liquidation price to act — by then options are very limited.

## 🟢 11. 6 Most Common Beginner Futures Mistakes

| Mistake | Description | Fix |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1. Too much leverage | Extremely small margin for error | Start low, e.g., 2x–5x |
| 2. Oversized position | Most capital on one trade | Risk 1–2% of total capital per trade |
| 3. No stop-loss | From "it'll come back" to "unacceptable loss" | Set stop-loss on every trade |
| 4. Adding to losers | Rapidly increases overall risk | Set clear add/no-add rules |
| 5. Small wins, big losses | Quick to close winners, hold losers forever | Set TP/SL ratio like 1:2 or 1:3 |
| 6. Treating rebates as risk control | Rebates only reduce costs | Rebates can't replace stop-loss, position sizing, or discipline |
