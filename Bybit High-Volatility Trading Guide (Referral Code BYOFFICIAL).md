## 1. What is high volatility?
Price movement speed increases sharply in a short time.

Example: BTC normally moves a few hundred dollars per hour, then suddenly pumps, dumps, or whipsaws hard.

---

## 2. The real problem is speed, not direction
New traders ask "up or down?".

But the harder part is: **price moves too fast.**

Entry, Stop Loss, Take Profit and position size may all need adjustment.

---

## 3. Normal position size may not fit high volatility
Same position can show PnL much faster during violent moves.

So many traders consider: **reduce size per trade.**

Not because they don't understand the market, but because same size = different risk under different volatility.

---

## 4. Wider stop loss is harder to set
- Stop too tight → knocked out by normal noise
- Stop too wide → larger loss per trade

Adjust: **Stop Loss distance + Position Size**
Don't just blindly widen the stop.

---

## 5. If stop widens, resize the position
Example:
- Original stop: 1%
- New stop: 2%

If size stays the same, max loss grows.

Better: **wider stop → smaller size**

Keep total risk in original range.

---

## 6. What is a Breakout?
Price moves beyond a key range.

Example: BTC ranges 68,000–70,000, then breaks 70,000 → potential upside breakout.

---

## 7. Breakout ≠ must chase immediately
After breakout:
1. Continuation
2. Pullback then continuation
3. Fake breakout back into range

---

## 8. What is a False Breakout?
Price breaks key level but fails to hold, then returns to range.

Example: BTC breaks 70,000, taps 70,300, falls back to 69,500. Late longs get trapped.

---

## 9. How to reduce chasing fake breakouts
No method avoids them completely, but reduce impulse:
- Does move continue after break?
- Does price snap back fast?
- Can it hold after pullback?
- Is volume or volatility stable?

For beginners: **don't chase every breakout immediately.**

---

## 10. What is a Pullback?
After breakout, price doesn't go straight.

Common: **breakout → pullback → decide direction**

---

## 11. Pullback doesn't mean automatic continuation
Check:
- support after pullback
- structure still intact
- not falling back into range
- risk still acceptable

Don't just think "it came back, so buy".

---

## 12. FOMO after pump
Fast +2%, +3%, +5% creates fear of missing out.

Often leads to:
- chasing highs
- oversizing
- higher leverage
- no stop loss

Risk escalates fast.

---

## 13. FOMO also happens on dump
"Already dropped so much, must bounce."

But you don't know the real bottom.

"Cheap" alone is not a complete reason to buy.

---

## 14. Market Order caution in high volatility
Market Order fills fast, but may suffer **slippage** — final price differs from seen price.

Larger market orders in fast markets need more care.

---

## 15. Limit Order advantage
You control acceptable price.

If price runs away, order simply doesn't fill.

Psychological benefit: **no forced chase.**

---

## 16. Limit may miss the move
That's the cost.

Limit trades execution chance for price control. Not always better than Market.

---

## 17. Futures + high volatility = leverage risk
If market already volatile, adding 20x, 50x or higher makes risk very sensitive.

**Volatility × leverage** hits the position together.

High volatility: reduce exposure, not raise leverage.

---

## 18. Liquidation price matters more
Higher leverage → liquidation price closer to market.

Liquidation triggers on **Mark Price**, not last traded price.

Don't treat liquidation price as your stop loss.

---

## 19. Long setup in high volatility
Ask:
1. Pre-breakout or chasing breakout?
2. Where is Stop Loss?
3. Can I handle instant pullback?
4. Did FOMO increase size?
5. Risk or Reward still valid?

If unclear, don't open.

---

## 20. Shorting is not just "too extended"
High volatility often:
- pumps more after big pump
- dumps more after big dump

"Overbought or oversold feeling" alone is not enough.

Need failed breakout, weak structure, stop level, sizing.

---

## 21. Waiting is a valid strategy
Big volatility doesn't mean you must trade.

If unclear: **don't trade.**

Error cost, slippage and emotional risk are higher.

---

## 22. Don't flip long or short repeatedly
Pump → long
pullback → close long
short
pump → close short → long again

That's usually no fixed logic.

---

## 23. Set "No Trade Condition"
Besides entry, preset conditions to skip trading:
- volatility spike in minutes
- major data just released
- consecutive pumps
- stop too far
- bad Risk or Reward
- emotional state bad

---

## 24. Scaling in high volatility
Scaling is fine, but not "keep adding on every drop".

Define:
- max entries
- size per entry
- total cap
- where to stop
- when to cancel plan

Otherwise scaling becomes unlimited averaging.

---

## 25. Trading cost can rise too
- wider spread
- more slippage
- more round trips
- emotional overtrades

Check cost, not just profit space.
