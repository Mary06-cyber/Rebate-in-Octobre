## 1. Understanding the Core Difference Between Isolated and Cross Margin

The starting point of position management is choosing the correct margin mode. In Isolated mode, each position has independent margin. When losses reach the maintenance margin level, only that position is liquidated without affecting other funds in the account. This suits beginners or users pursuing precise single-trade risk control. In Cross mode, the entire account available balance serves as collateral for all positions. It offers high flexibility but amplifies risk — if the market turns against you, the entire account could be dragged down.

| Mode | Feature | Liquidation Impact | Best For |
|------|---------|-------------------|----------|
| Isolated | Independent margin, risk isolation | Only loses allocated margin | Beginners, single-trade control |
| Cross | Shares entire account balance | May affect whole futures account | Experienced users, hedgers |

It is recommended to choose based on your risk tolerance and dynamically adjust positions by manually adding or reducing margin at critical moments. For contract newcomers, starting with Isolated mode is strongly advised to lock single-trade losses within an affordable range.

## 2. Rational Combination of Leverage and Position Size

Leverage is a double-edged sword. Binance advises beginners to start with low leverage of 2x to 5x, which moderately amplifies returns while effectively controlling liquidation risk. For position sizing, the industry generally follows the principle of risking no more than 1% to 2% of total capital per trade.

Example: Account total asset is 1000 USDT. Maximum single-trade loss should be controlled within 10 to 20 USDT. Assuming a stop distance of 100 points, each point risk is 0.1 to 0.2 USDT. Calculate the appropriate opening lot accordingly. Never go all-in. Even with high leverage, participate with small positions and leave sufficient risk buffer space.

Remember, high leverage does not equal high win rate. Many beginners mistakenly believe 100x leverage leads to quick riches; in reality, it only accelerates liquidation. Steady position sizing combined with moderate leverage is the foundation of long-term profitability.

## 3. Making Good Use of TP/SL and Risk Buffer Tools

No position management can function without disciplined risk control tools. Binance supports Take Profit (TP) and Stop Loss (SL) orders. You can preset execution prices when opening a position or during holding to automatically lock in profits or prevent expanding losses. Additionally, Binance offers a Risk Margin feature, allowing you to transfer part of your assets as an extra buffer to reduce liquidation probability.

- Stop Loss Order: Set a trigger price below market price. When reached, automatically closes at market or limit price, cutting losses.
- Take Profit Order: Set a trigger price above market price. Automatically takes profit when target is reached.
- Risk Margin: Transfer extra funds to increase margin ratio and prevent unexpected liquidation.
- Auto-Transfer Margin: When margin ratio hits a risk threshold, the system automatically supplements funds to avoid forced closure.
- Trailing Stop: Automatically follows the stop loss level as price moves favorably, locking in floating profits.

## 4. Advanced Position Management Strategies

When trends are clear, use the Pyramid Adding method: add positions gradually during pullbacks instead of going heavy all at once, thereby averaging down holding costs while controlling risk. Specifically, allocate a larger portion to the first position, then decrease each subsequent add, avoiding excessive bottom-heavy exposure.

Before major data releases (such as CPI, FOMC decisions, Non-Farm Payroll), proactively lower leverage to below 3x and lock risk with Isolated mode. At key technical breakout points, moderately use 5x Cross with floating stops. Also diversify fund allocation — for example, follow the 50-30-20 rule: 50% for spot reserves, 30% for low-risk wealth management or staking, and only 10% to 20% for low-leverage contracts. Never put all eggs in one basket.

## ❓ FAQ

**Q1: How to choose between Binance Isolated and Cross margin modes?**
A: Isolated gives each position independent margin; losses only affect that position and liquidation won't drag other funds. It suits beginners or those seeking precise single-trade control. Cross uses the entire account balance as collateral — flexible but higher overall risk. Beginners should start with Isolated, then switch to Cross as experience grows.

**Q2: What leverage should beginners use for Binance futures?**
A: Binance recommends starting with 2x to 5x low leverage to limit risk and avoid liquidation during violent market swings. High leverage amplifies both gains and losses. Beginners should first get familiar with market rhythm at low leverage, then gradually build position management skills before considering higher multiples.

**Q3: How to set TP/SL on Binance to control position risk?**
A: Binance supports Take Profit (TP) and Stop Loss (SL) orders. While holding a position, set TP above market price to lock profits and SL below to cap losses. You can set them when opening or adjust anytime. Proper use of TP/SL is the most critical disciplined risk control in position management.

**Q4: What is Binance Risk Margin feature?**
A: Risk Margin is a buffer tool. Users can transfer part of their assets as extra collateral to increase margin ratio and reduce liquidation probability. Enabling Auto-Transfer Margin lets the system automatically add funds when margin ratio hits a high-risk threshold, preventing unexpected forced closure.

**Q5: How much risk per trade in position management?**
A: Industry standard is risking no more than 1% to 2% of total capital per trade. For a 1000 USDT account, max single-trade loss should be 10 to 20 USDT. Calculate opening lot and stop distance accordingly. This way, even after consecutive losses, sufficient capital remains for continued trading.

**Q6: What is Pyramid Adding and how to operate it?**
A: Pyramid Adding means not going all-in at one price, but gradually adding during pullbacks with decreasing size each time. Example: first add 500 units, next 300, then 150. This averages cost while controlling risk, avoiding the passivity of a single heavy entry.

**Q7: How to adjust positions before major news events?**

When trends are clear and volatility rises, moderately use 5x Cross with floating stops for balanced offense and defense, avoiding liquidation from sudden events.
