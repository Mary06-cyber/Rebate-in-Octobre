## 4. What are Bid and Ask?

On the trading panel you usually see:

**Bid**: price buyers are willing to pay.
**Ask**: price sellers are willing to accept.

Example:

highest bid: 99.9
lowest ask: 100.1

The gap is:

Spread｜bid-ask spread

Spread is also a hidden cost, especially for low-liquidity pairs or volatile moments.

---

## 5. Why Spread is also a cost

Many people only look at trading fee, but spread affects execution too.

Example:

you buy at the higher Ask,
then immediately sell at the lower Bid.

Even if price did not move much, you may still have a small loss.

That is why just getting filled does not mean closing at the same price is break-even.

---

## 6. When Spread can be wider

Pay more attention when:

- market moves violently
- trading pair is illiquid
- low-activity hours
- sudden news
- large clustered orders

Do not pick pairs only by top gainers. Check liquidity too.

---

## 7. What is Slippage?

Slippage is the difference between expected fill price and actual average fill price.

Example:

you see price: 100
click Market Buy
final average fill: 100.2

That difference is part of slippage.

---

## 8. Why Market Orders slip more easily

Market Order prioritizes execution.
If the current price level does not have enough size, the order continues to the next levels.

Example:

you want to buy 1,000 USDT,
but first level only has small size.
Remaining size may fill at:

100.1
100.2
100.3

So average fill can be higher than the first price you saw.

---

## 10. Limit Order helps control price

Limit Order does not guarantee profit.
Its main use is controlling the price you are willing to accept.

Example:

BTC is too high now, you only want to buy lower.
Set a Limit.
If price never comes, it does not fill.

---

## 11. But Limit has another issue

It may never fill.

You wait for lower price, market runs away, your Limit stays open.

So Limit trades execution chance for price control.

---

## 12. Market vs Limit

Market: faster execution.
Limit: better price control.

If entry price matters a lot, Limit is usually easier to control.
If you need to exit fast, Market may be more practical.

Neither is always better.

## 15. Futures also have Funding Rate

If you trade futures, besides fees you may need:

Funding Rate

It is not a Bitget platform fee. It is exchanged between longs and shorts in perpetual contracts to keep contract price close to spot index price.

Commonly settled every 8 hours. You only pay or receive if you hold position at settlement time.

Formula:

Funding Fee = Position Value × Funding Rate

Positive rate: long pays short.
Negative rate: short pays long.

For swing or longer futures holds, do not only look at Entry and Target. Position holding has extra cost too.
