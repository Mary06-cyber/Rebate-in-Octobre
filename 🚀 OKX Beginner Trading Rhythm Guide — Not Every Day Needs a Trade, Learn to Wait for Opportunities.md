## 1. The Real Beginner Problem Is Not Lack of Opportunities, But Too Many

After opening OKX, you might see:

BTC moving
ETH moving
SOL moving
Futures moving
New coins moving
Lots of market news

Easy to feel: "If I don't act now, I'll miss out."

But the market will always have the next fluctuation.

What to truly avoid: trading without a plan just because you fear missing out.

## 2. Categorize Opportunities Into 3 Types

A｜Clear Opportunity
Meets your preset conditions.
Example: price at planned level, clear trend structure, risk controllable, clear exit point. Worth serious consideration.

B｜Vague Opportunity
Looks like it might work, but: direction unclear, hard to place SL, price already ran too far, poor risk-reward. Observe first.

C｜Emotional Opportunity
Example: FOMO, just saw a big pump/dump, someone said it'll moon, want to recover last loss. Most likely to cause loss of control.

## 3. Don't Mistake "Volatility" for "Opportunity"

Fast market movement does not mean it's a good time to enter.

Example: BTC suddenly pumps. Many's first reaction: chase Long.

Better to ask:

Did I already miss the better entry?
If I chase now, how far does my SL need to be?
If it pulls back immediately, can I accept that?
Is the risk-reward still reasonable?

If you can't answer these, maybe don't chase.

## 4. Learn to Stay in Cash (No Position)

Many beginners feel: no position = doing nothing. Wrong.

No position means: no market risk exposure, can wait for better prices, not affected by floating P&L emotions, can re-evaluate the market.

So: staying in cash is an active choice.

## 5. You Don't Need to Check Charts Dozens of Times Daily

If you refresh constantly, try: fixed-time checks.

Example:

First time: overall market direction
Second time: any prices at planned levels
Third time: if holding positions, manage risk

No need to refresh every few minutes. Reducing screen time can improve judgment quality.

## 6. What You Should Do Is Write in Advance, Not Just Watch More

Instead of staring at charts, write down in advance:

What to watch today?
Example: BTC, ETH, SOL

At what price to consider? Mark zones beforehand.

When NOT to trade?
Example: already pumped fast, too volatile, SL too far, no clear direction.

Write it down, and you won't panic during live action.

## 8. Buying BTC, ETH, SOL Simultaneously Does Not Equal Diversification

When the market is strong, they rise together. Many think buying all three = diversified.

But if they fall together, you'll find: you just multiplied the same directional risk several times.

Multiple positions don't always mean true diversification. Sometimes it's just: repeated exposure.

## 9. Set a "Max Trades Per Day" Limit

Example: max 2 trades.

If today's first two are done, don't add impulsive trades just because the chart keeps moving.

This limit helps reduce: overtrading, emotional trading, revenge trading, unplanned chasing.

## 10. Don't Suddenly Speed Up After Consecutive Wins

Not just losses cause loss of control. Consecutive wins do too.

Example: after 3 wins, easy to think "I'm hot today, let me size up." Then next trade becomes: bigger size, higher leverage, looser SL, relaxed rules.

So: after winning streaks, maintain original rhythm.

## 11. After Consecutive Losses, Lower Frequency Even More

If you lose 2-3 in a row, don't immediately increase trade frequency.

Better approach: pause, review past trades, check if repeating same mistakes, see if market conditions changed.

Don't use more trades to solve previous losses.

## 12. Market Orders Most Easily Cause Chasing

Market order's biggest advantage: speed.

But because it's so fast, beginners easily hit it as soon as they see movement.

Before using Market, ask: "If I couldn't fill immediately, would I still want this trade?"

If answer is "not sure," you're likely just being dragged by short-term volatility.

## 13. Limit Orders Help You Practice Waiting

If you have a planned price, Limit is better.

Example: BTC hasn't reached your zone yet, set a Limit order. If it gets there, it fills. If not, no harm done.

This helps practice: not chasing price.

## 14. Stop Loss Is Not Just to Prevent Big Losses, But to End a Trade

Many find opening positions easy, but hardest is: not knowing when to admit the trade is over.

Stop Loss defines in advance: if price reaches here, this trade stops.

That's more disciplined than constantly thinking "maybe it'll come back."

## 15. Take Profit Controls Greed

After profit, easy to think "wait, maybe more." Then +50 becomes +30, then +10, then 0.

Take Profit's role: handles greed in advance.

## 16. After Each Day, Review Only 3 Things

No need for long journals. Daily ask yourself:

1｜Did I take any unplanned trades today?
2｜Did I chase due to FOMO?
3｜Did I violate my SL or position rules?

If you can control these three for several days straight, trading rhythm usually improves significantly.
