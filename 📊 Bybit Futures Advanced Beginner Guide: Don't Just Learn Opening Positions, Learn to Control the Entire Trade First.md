## 1️⃣ Break Down a Trade into 4 Parts Before Trading Futures

A complete futures trade can be broken down into:

**1｜Why enter**
Why do you want to make this trade? Because of trend breakout, pullback to support, reversal at resistance, range trading, other clear conditions — not because "it feels like it will rise."

**2｜What if wrong**
If the market doesn't move in your direction, where does it indicate your judgment has failed? This is your Stop Loss.

**3｜Where to take profit if right**
If direction is correct, where to reduce position, take profit, close all? Think ahead.

**4｜Maximum loss for this trade**
This is the most important step. Don't ask "how much can I make?" first, but ask "if I'm wrong, what's the maximum I'm willing to lose?"

## 2️⃣ Why Position Size Matters More Than Leverage

Many beginners focus heavily on 5x, 10x, 20x, 50x, but leverage alone is not enough. What truly affects account risk is: leverage × position size × stop distance. For example, two traders both using 10x: one opens a tiny position, the other puts in a large amount of funds. Their actual risks are completely different. So don't just ask "is 10x high?" Also look at "how big is this position actually?"

## 3️⃣ Beginners Should Learn "Fixed Risk" First, Not "Amplifying Profits"

Assume your account has 1,000 USDT. You can first set a maximum loss of 10-20 USDT per trade, then calculate position size based on entry price, stop distance, leverage, and position. This is more logical than "I have 1000U, so I'll open a 500U position." A fixed risk model helps you survive longer in the market.

## 4️⃣ Long and Short Are Not Just Guessing Up or Down

**Long**: Not because "this coin has dropped a lot, should rise," but because you have a clear bullish logic, such as price reclaiming a key level, support appearing after pullback, trend turning strong again.

**Short**: Not because "it has risen so much, must fall," but with clear bearish conditions, such as failure to break resistance, breaking key support, weakening rebound structure.

The biggest fear in futures trading is not being wrong once, but trading continuously without logic, relying only on feelings.

## 5️⃣ Decide "Where Not to Trade" Before "Where to Trade"

Many beginners only look for entry points, like "BTC reaches here I'll go long." But a more mature mindset also determines: under what conditions will I not trade? For example, price has already surged too fast, stop distance too far, poor risk-reward ratio, sudden abnormal volatility, unclear direction. Sometimes, not opening a position is itself a trading decision.

## 6️⃣ Market Order Is Not Everything

**Market Order**: Suitable when you need quick execution. Fast and simple, but may have slippage during violent fluctuations.

**Limit Order**: Suitable when you already have a clear entry zone. For example, if you only want to go long near a certain support, you can place an order in advance. Easier to control entry price, no need to chase, but if price doesn't reach, it won't execute.

## 7️⃣ Don't Just Stare at "Entry Price", Also Look at These 4 Numbers

After opening a futures position, focus on:

1. **Average Entry Price**: your actual holding cost.
2. **Current Price**: where the market is now.
3. **Stop Loss Level**: where to exit if wrong.
4. **Liquidation Price**: where position may be forcibly closed if margin is insufficient.

Many only look at "how much I'm making now," but ignore how far from stop loss or liquidation. This is a very dangerous habit.

## 8️⃣ Profitable Trades Also Need Management

Many think risk control is only needed when losing. Actually, profitable trades also need management. For example, if a trade is already in profit, consider partial profit-taking, trailing stop, holding part of position, closing all as planned, rather than "already making money, wait a bit more, maybe it can double." Unplanned profits can turn back into losses.

## 9️⃣ Why "Trailing Stop" Needs Rules

Trailing stop itself is not a problem. The problem is many people do this: price nears stop → move stop back → nears again → move further back. This is not risk management, but "unwilling to accept loss." A truly reasonable trailing stop should be: after price has moved favorably, adjust to protect profits, not to escape stop loss.

## 🔟 After Consecutive Losses, Don't Double Down

Assume you lose 3 trades in a row. Many think "next trade I'll increase size to recover." This easily turns into Revenge Trading. A more reasonable approach: pause trading, check if recent trades made the same mistake, reduce size, wait for truly qualified opportunities. After consecutive losses, what needs recovery most is "judgment," not immediate profit recovery.

## 1️⃣1️⃣ Don't Use "Adding to Position" as a Stop Loss Substitute

Many keep adding after losses: first loss, add a bit; drop more, add more; continue dropping, add again; finally forming a huge position. Adding can be a strategy, but only if designed before opening. For example, know in advance maximum adds, size per add, total risk limit, where to exit completely. If just because "already losing, don't want to leave," that's usually not a strategy.

## 1️⃣2️⃣ The Most Overlooked in Futures: Trading Frequency

Many trade 10, 20 times or more a day. The problem isn't just fees, but also judgment fatigue, emotional swings, overtrading, continuous chasing, increasingly casual plans. So futures trading isn't necessarily "more trades, more opportunities." Sometimes, "fewer trades, stricter selection."

## 1️⃣4️⃣ Beginners' First Stage: No Need for Complex Strategies

Start with 3 things:

1. **Fixed risk per trade**: e.g., only bear a small portion of account loss per trade.
2. **Stop loss for every trade**: no trading without exit plan.
3. **Review after each trade**: record why in, why out, what went wrong, if plan violated. Stabilize execution before studying complex indicators.
