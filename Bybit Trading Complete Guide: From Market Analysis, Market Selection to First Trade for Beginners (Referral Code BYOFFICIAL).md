Spot

Futures

2. Beginners should first understand the trading pair

For example, seeing BTCUSDT: The BTC in front is the asset you are trading. The USDT behind is the pricing asset. Simply understand: How much USDT is BTC worth now.

Similarly: ETHUSDT means ETH against USDT. SOLUSDT means SOL against USDT.

If you are still a beginner, there is no need to look at many trading pairs at once. Getting familiar with BTCUSDT is usually enough.

3. Check 3 things before entering the market

Before preparing to trade, do not buy as soon as you see the price.

1 | Direction: Is the recent price rising, falling, or moving sideways?
2 | Position: Is the price now just breaking out, pulling back, in the middle of a range, or has it already risen a lot consecutively?
3 | Volatility: Is the market now stable, normal, or suddenly fluctuating rapidly?

These three things will directly affect whether you should enter now.

4. Do not place an order when you are most excited

The easiest time for many beginners to actually trade for the first time is usually when BTC suddenly surges. Because at this time they think: "If I don't buy now, it will be too late." But what really needs to be confirmed first is: Has it already risen a lot? Am I chasing the price by entering now? If there is a pullback, can I accept it? Where is my exit position? If there is no answer at all, do not rush to place an order.

5. If doing Spot, the first trade can be completed like this

Suppose you are ready to buy BTC. You can first enter BTCUSDT Spot. Then decide: How much USDT are you going to use? For example: 100 USDT. Next, you need to choose: Market or Limit.

6. How to understand Market Order?

Market means: execute the order as soon as possible at the current market available price. Suitable for: wanting to enter immediately, not wanting to wait for price, small transaction amount, normal market liquidity. For example: BTC is now at a position you can accept, and you want to buy directly, you can consider Market. But note: when the market fluctuates rapidly, the actual execution price may be slightly different from the price you just saw.

7. How to use Limit Order?

Limit is: you set the price you want to execute at. For example: BTC is now at 68,000. But you only want to buy around 67,000. Then you can set a Limit. If the price returns to your set position, the order has a chance to be executed. If it never reaches, the order may never be executed.

8. How to choose between Market and Limit?

Simply remember: Want immediate execution → Market. Want to wait for a price → Limit. For beginners, what you really need to avoid is not which one to use, but pressing down without any price concept.

9. Do not just stare at floating profit and loss after buying

Many people start staring at +3, +8, -5, +12 after buying, and their emotions change with the numbers. But what you should really do is: return to the original trading plan first. For example: If it rises to where, will I consider selling? If it falls to where, will I admit that my judgment was wrong? These two questions are more important than constantly staring at floating profit.

10. Spot also needs an exit plan

Many people think Spot does not need a stop loss. In fact, whether to use Stop Loss can be decided according to your trading style, but you must have an exit logic. For example, you can set in advance: Sell in batches when reaching the target price; Reduce position if breaking below a certain structure; Hold until a certain time before re-evaluating. Do not become: completely not knowing when to sell after buying.

11. If you want to do Futures, do not directly choose high leverage first

After entering Futures, many people see at first glance: 5x, 10x, 20x, 50x. The easiest thought to produce is: "The higher the multiple, the faster the profit." But leverage also amplifies risk at the same time. So a more reasonable order is: Decide direction first → decide stop loss → decide position → finally look at leverage. Not: choose 20x first, then think about what to do.

12. What is Long?

Long = bullish. If you think BTC may rise later, you can consider Long. For example: Entry 67,000, Target 69,000. If the direction meets expectations, the price goes up, Long will generate profit.

13. What is Short?

Short = bearish. If you think BTC may fall later, you can consider Short. For example: Entry 69,000, Target 67,000. If the price goes down, Short may generate profit. But note: Short is not necessarily doing short just because "it has risen a lot." Direction still needs your own trading reason.

14. Fill in 4 numbers before Futures trading

If you are ready to do Futures, fill in before placing an order: Entry (Where to enter), Stop Loss (Where to exit if wrong), Take Profit (Where to handle if right), Position Size (How large a position to use this time). If all four are not figured out yet, it is not recommended to rush to open a position.

15. Why is Stop Loss so important?

Because the biggest problem with Futures is not being wrong once. But: after being wrong, the loss has no upper limit. The function of Stop Loss is: tell yourself in advance that if the market reaches here, this trade ends. This way you do not need to wait until emotional breakdown, too much floating loss, close to liquidation, to start handling.

16. Take Profit should also be planned in advance

Many beginners set Stop Loss in advance, but do not have Take Profit. As a result, after the market really goes right, they start thinking: "Can it be more?" In the end, they may give back most of the floating profit. So you can consider in advance: Sell part at the first target; Handle at the second target; Or exit all at once. Having rules is better than making temporary decisions.

17. What is the difference between Isolated and Cross?

When doing Futures, you may see: Isolated Margin (a certain position uses relatively independent margin, usually more convenient to control the risk of a single trade) and Cross Margin (more available margin in the account may jointly bear the risk). If you do not really understand the difference between the two, beginners should not switch casually. Especially in high leverage environments, margin mode will directly affect risk.

18. Do not pursue making a lot of money in the first Futures trade

The first time you do Futures, what you really need to learn is not making 100U in one trade. But to be familiar with the complete process: Choose trading pair → Check direction → Decide Entry → Set Stop Loss → Set Take Profit → Control position → Open position → Observe → Close position. If you can do it completely once, it is more valuable than simply looking at how much you earned in the end.

19. Do not open many trading pairs at the same time

Many people start to look at BTC, ETH, SOL, DOGE, XRP, other coins at the same time. In the end: want to do every one. Beginners are more suitable to first choose 1-2 main trading pairs. After getting familiar with price rhythm, volatility, order placement, take profit and stop loss, then slowly increase.
