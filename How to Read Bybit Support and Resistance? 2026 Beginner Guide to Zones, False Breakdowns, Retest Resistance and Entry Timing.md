## 1. What Is Support?

Support can be simply understood as:

A zone where obvious buying reaction previously appeared when price moved downward.

For example:

BTC drops from 70,000 to around 68,000, and bounces multiple times near 68,000.

Then the area near 68,000 may form a support zone.

But note: support does not guarantee a bounce.

---

## 2. What Is Resistance?

Resistance can be understood as:

A zone where obvious selling pressure previously appeared when price moved upward.

For example:

BTC rises multiple times toward 72,000 and then pulls back.

Then the area near 72,000 may form a resistance zone.

Similarly: resistance does not guarantee a reversal downward.

---

## 3. Why Should Support and Resistance Be Seen as Zones?

Many beginners like to draw one extremely precise line.

For example: 68,000.

Then they believe: as soon as price goes below 68,000, support completely fails.

But real market transactions do not stop exactly at 68,000.00 every time.

Price may hit 67,950, 67,880, or 68,050 before recovering.

So a more reasonable understanding is: Support Zone, not an absolute number.

---

## 4. Why Should the Zone Not Be Too Wide?

Although support is better viewed as a zone, you cannot mark everything from 66,000 to 69,000 as support.

If the zone is too wide, it loses practical reference value.

A better approach is to observe:

Areas with repeated obvious reactions
Candle cluster zones
Previous highs and lows
Clear volume turning points

Then draw a relatively reasonable price band.

---

## 5. Does More Tests Mean Stronger Support?

Not necessarily.

Many people think: the more times support is tested, the more reliable it is.

But in reality, each test may consume local buying power.

For example:

First test → strong bounce
Second test → weaker bounce
Third test → almost no reaction

This may actually indicate: support is weakening.

---

## 6. The Same Applies to Resistance

If price hits resistance the first time and drops quickly.

The second time it approaches, the pullback is smaller.

The third time it starts hugging the resistance level.

That may indicate: selling pressure is being absorbed.

At that point, you cannot just keep shorting because "this used to be resistance."

---

## 7. What Does Support Failure Look Like?

Support failure is not necessarily one瞬间 price spike below the level.

More importantly: can price reclaim the zone after breaking below?

For example:

Support zone: 68,000

Price briefly drops to 67,800 but quickly returns to 68,300.

This may just be a false breakdown.

But if price stays below support after breaking down, and even failed rallies cannot reclaim it, then support failure is more likely.

---

## 8. What Is a False Breakdown?

False Breakdown means:

Price briefly breaks below support but quickly returns above the support zone.

For example:

Support near 68,000.

Price suddenly drops to 67,700, looking like it will keep falling.

Then it quickly rallies back to 68,400.

The breakdown did not actually sustain.

---

## 9. Why Do False Breakdowns Confuse Beginners?

Because many beginners see one breakdown and immediately short.

Result: price quickly reclaims support, and the short entry becomes terrible.

So after seeing a breakdown, do not only ask: "Did it break?"

Also ask: "Did it stay broken?"

---

## 10. What Is Retest Resistance?

Suppose original support was at 68,000.

Later it gets genuinely broken.

Price drops to 67,000, then rallies back toward 68,000.

If 68,000 now acts as pressure and pushes price down again, this is called:

Original support turning into resistance.

This structure is very common.

---

## 11. What Is Breakout Retest?

The reverse is also true.

Original resistance at 72,000.

Price genuinely breaks out, rises to 73,000.

Then pulls back toward 72,000.

If the old resistance now acts as support and price moves up again, this is:

Breakout Retest.

---

## 12. Why Is Breakout Retest Sometimes Easier to Judge Than Chasing?

Because when you chase the breakout immediately, you often do not know:

Is it a real breakout or a fake one?

A retest gives you more information.

For example:

Did the breakout zone hold?
Did old resistance turn into support?
Is the stop loss clearer?
Did the risk/reward improve?

So many times: waiting for the retest is easier for risk control than chasing immediately.

---

## 13. But Not Every Breakout Retests

This is also important.

Some breakouts accelerate directly after breaking out, with no clear pullback.

If you insist on waiting for price to come back, you may miss the move.

But missing is not the same as being wrong.

Because: not entering is often easier to manage than forcing an entry at an unreasonable location.

---

## 14. Is It Always Fine to Long Near Support?

No.

Support is only a zone that needs careful observation.

Not an automatic buy button.

Near support you also need to check:

Is there buying reaction?
Did price stop making lower lows?
Did it reclaim the key zone?
Is there a reasonable stop loss?

If there is no reaction at all, support may be failing.

---

## 15. Is It Always Fine to Short Near Resistance?

Also no.

If price keeps testing resistance, each pullback gets weaker, it stays near the high longer, and lows keep getting higher, that may indicate bulls are continuously absorbing selling pressure.

Blindly shorting may get caught in a real breakout.

---

## 16. What Is Higher Support?

Suppose BTC support was originally at 68,000.

Later price rises.

The next pullback only reaches 69,500 before moving up again.

This may indicate: the market is willing to buy at higher prices.

In other words: support is moving up.

This is usually a sign of relatively strong structure.

---

## 17. What Is Lower Resistance?

Conversely, if:

First bounce reaches 72,000.
Second only reaches 71,000.
Third only reaches 70,500.

That may indicate: selling pressure appears at lower levels.

Resistance keeps moving down, which usually means: market structure is weakening.

---

## 18. Support and Resistance Can Also Help Judge Trend

If: supports get higher and resistances get higher, it usually leans toward an uptrend structure.

If: supports get lower and resistances get lower, it usually leans toward a downtrend structure.

So support and resistance are not just for finding entries. They also help understand market structure.

---

## 19. Do Not Draw Too Many Lines on the Chart

Many beginners end up with a chart showing 10, 20, or 30 lines.

Result: every price level can be explained by some line.

This loses judgment value.

A simpler approach is to keep only:

Recent important highs
Recent important lows
Clear consolidation zones
Major breakout areas

Usually that is enough.

---

## 20. Which Support and Resistance Levels Are More Worth Watching?

Prioritize:

1. Areas with repeated obvious reactions
2. Large timeframe highs and lows
3. Upper and lower boundaries of long consolidation zones
4. Positions where important breakouts just occurred
5. Areas where price reversed quickly

Not every small fluctuation needs to become a support or resistance line.

---

## 21. Larger Timeframe Support and Resistance Deserve More Attention

For example, a small high on the 5-minute chart may only affect short-term moves.

But important zones on the 4-hour or daily chart are watched by far more traders.

So a simple rule:

Draw main zones on larger timeframes, find finer entries on smaller timeframes.

---

## 22. Do Not Go All-In Just Because Price Touches Support

Even if you think: "This is an important support."

It does not guarantee it will hold.

So you still need:

Position size
Stop loss
Maximum risk limit

Support and resistance are judgment tools, not guarantee tools.

---

## 23. Do Not Immediately Add to a Position After Support Breaks

Many people buy at support originally.

Then support breaks.

First reaction: "It is cheaper now, let me add more."

But if support has genuinely failed, then the original trade reasoning may have also failed.

At that point, re-evaluate instead of simply adding more risk just because price is lower.

---

## 24. After Breakout, Do Not Place Stop Loss Too Close

If price just broke out, a normal retest may pull back to the breakout zone.

If your stop is too close, you may get stopped out before the structure actually fails.

So stops should be based on market structure, not just how much percentage you personally want to risk.

---

## 25. Support and Resistance Can Also Help Set Take Profit

For example:

You go long near support.

Then the nearest major resistance ahead can serve as the first take-profit reference zone.

Conversely:

Short near resistance.

The major support below can become the target zone.

This is usually more structurally logical than randomly setting a percentage.

---

## 26. Risk/Reward Must Be Judged Together With Support and Resistance

For example:

Entry at 69,500
Stop at 68,800
Front resistance at 69,900

Then upside space is very small.

Even if directionally bullish, the risk/reward of this trade may still be poor.

So: having support does not automatically mean the trade is worth taking.

You also need to check: how much room is left ahead.

---

## 27. What Is the Relationship Between Support/Resistance and Liquidity?

Near important highs and lows, there is usually concentration of:

Stop loss orders
Breakout orders
Limit orders
Long and short positions

So when price approaches these levels, volatility sometimes expands suddenly.

This is also why key zones often see false breakouts and false breakdowns.

---

## 28. Do Not Judge Breakout Failure Based on One Wick Alone

One long wick does not necessarily mean reversal.

It may just be short-term volatility.

More worth observing:

Did subsequent candles show continuation?
Did price return inside the zone?
Did market structure actually change?

So: a single candle is only one piece of information.

## FAQ

Q1: Should Bybit support and resistance be drawn as one line or a zone?
Usually better as a zone, because market price rarely reverses at exactly the same number every time.

Q2: Does support breaking mean price will definitely keep falling?
Not necessarily. Sometimes a false breakdown occurs, where price quickly reclaims the support zone after briefly breaking it.

Q3: Should I buy immediately after resistance breaks?
Not necessarily. Breakouts can also fail. Observe whether price truly holds above, and whether a breakout retest occurs.

Q4: Is support more reliable the more times it is tested?
Not necessarily. If each bounce gets weaker, it may indicate support is being consumed.

Q5: Can support and resistance be used to set take profit?
They can serve as reference. For example, when going long, major resistance ahead can be a take-profit zone.
