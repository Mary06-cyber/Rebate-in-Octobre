So if you have no trading experience, get familiar with Spot first, then study Futures.

## 2. What is BTCUSDT?

Beginners seeing BTCUSDT for the first time may find it complicated. Actually, it is very simple.

BTC is: the asset you are trading.
USDT is: the asset used for pricing.
BTCUSDT can be understood as: how much USDT 1 BTC is worth now.

Similarly:
ETHUSDT = ETH against USDT
SOLUSDT = SOL against USDT

If you are just starting to learn trading, just understand BTCUSDT first.

## 3. Before your first trade, check what the price is doing

Do not click buy immediately after opening the trading page.
Check three things first:

### 1｜Direction
Is the price:
- Going up
- Going down
- Or moving sideways

### 2｜Position
Is the price:
- Just breaking out
- Pulling back
- In the middle of a range
- Or already pumped a lot

### 3｜Volatility
Is the market:
- Relatively stable
- Normally volatile
- Or suddenly pumping/dumping fast

Especially when the market pumps suddenly, do not chase just because you are afraid of missing out.

## 5. What is Market Order?

Market Order means executing the trade as soon as possible at the current market price.
For example, if you think the current BTC price is acceptable and want to buy now, you can choose Market.

Pros:
- Simple operation
- Fast execution
- Easy for beginners to understand

But note: if the market is volatile, the actual fill price may differ from the price you just saw.

## 6. What is Limit Order?

Limit Order means setting the price you want to buy or sell.
For example, BTC is currently at 68,000 USDT, but you only want to buy at 67,000. You can set the Limit price to 67,000.
If the market returns to this price, the order may be filled. If the price never reaches it, the order may not be filled.

## 7. How to choose between Market and Limit?

Simply remember:
Want to trade now → Market
Want to wait for your price → Limit

Both are very common. What really matters is: know why you are trading at this price before placing the order. Do not click just because the price moves.

## 8. What to do after buying Spot?

Many beginners are best at buying, but worst at selling.
After buying BTC, do not just look at +10, +20, -10, +30.
Think ahead:
- Where to take profit?
- Where to re-evaluate if it drops?
- How long do I plan to hold?

A trade is only complete with an exit plan.

## 9. Can I buy Spot in installments?

Yes.
For example, you prepared 300 USDT to buy BTC. You do not have to buy all at once. You can split it into: 100 USDT / 100 USDT / 100 USDT.
Whether to split depends on your strategy. The key is not to keep buying the dip blindly, but every add-on should have pre-set conditions.

## 10. How to trade Bybit Futures?

If you are familiar with the basic buying and selling process, start looking at Futures.
Futures trading mainly requires understanding Long and Short first.

## 11. What is Long?

Long means you expect the price to rise.
For example: BTC Entry 67,000, you expect it to rise to 69,000, you can study Long.
If the price goes up, Long may generate profit. If the price goes down, it may generate loss.

## 12. What is Short?

Short means you expect the price to fall.
For example: BTC Entry 69,000, you think it will drop to 67,000 later, you can study Short.
If the price drops, Short may generate profit. If the price continues to rise, it may generate loss.

## 13. Rising a lot does not automatically mean Short

This is a common beginner mistake. Seeing BTC rise a lot and thinking "it has risen so much, it should drop" then shorting directly.
Similarly, seeing a big drop, do not just think "it has dropped so much, it should rebound" and go Long directly.
Trading direction should come from: market structure, price position, your own setup, and whether the risk is reasonable. Not simply because it has risen or fallen too much.

## 14. Leverage is not the most important thing in Futures

Many people look at 5x, 10x, 20x, 50x first when they first see Futures. But the truly reasonable order should be:
1. Decide Entry first
2. Decide Stop Loss
3. Decide position size
4. Consider leverage last
Not: choose 50x first, then think about how to trade.

## 15. What is Stop Loss?

Stop Loss means setting in advance: if the price reaches this point, this trade ends.
Stop Loss's main function is not to predict the market, but to limit the risk of a single trade.

## 16. What is Take Profit?

Take Profit means setting in advance: where to start exiting when in profit.
For example: close part at the first target, close part at the second target, or exit all at the target.
Setting Take Profit in advance avoids constantly changing your mind after being in profit.

## 17. Write these 4 numbers before opening a Futures position

Before placing a Futures order, fill in:
- Entry (entry price)
- Stop Loss (stop loss price)
- Take Profit (take profit price)
- Position Size (position size)

If none of these four are clear, do not rush to open a position.

## 18. How to understand Isolated and Cross?

In Futures trading you may see:
### Isolated Margin
Can be understood as: separately managing the margin risk of a single position.
### Cross Margin
More available margin may jointly support the position.
The risk structures of the two modes are different. If you do not truly understand, do not switch casually just because you see others using it.

## 19. What is Liquidation?

When trading Futures, because margin and leverage are used, if the loss reaches a certain level, the position may face Liquidation (forced close).
So do not treat the liquidation price as a normal stop loss price. It is more reasonable to set Stop Loss in advance to keep risk within an acceptable range.
