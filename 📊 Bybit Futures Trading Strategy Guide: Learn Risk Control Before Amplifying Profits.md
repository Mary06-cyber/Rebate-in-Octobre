## 1️⃣ Answer 4 Questions Before Every Futures Trade

Think through these four questions before placing an order. This helps build a complete trading plan and avoid emotional decisions.

**1｜Why enter?**
Entry reasons should be as clear as possible, such as: trend breakout, pullback to support, resistance rejection, range trading, preset technical conditions. Not just "feeling it will rise soon."

**2｜What if the judgment is wrong?**
You need to find a position where the trading logic fails in advance. This is usually the reference point for Stop Loss.

**3｜What if the judgment is right?**
Plan ahead: where to take profit first, whether to reduce position in batches, how to handle remaining positions, and where to fully exit. Don't decide temporarily after profits appear.

**4｜What is the maximum loss?**
This is more important than "how much can I make?" Determine the maximum acceptable loss first, then decide position size.

## 2️⃣ What Truly Determines Risk Is Not Just Leverage

Many people only stare at 5x, 10x, 20x, 50x, but leverage alone is not enough. Actual risk also relates to position size + stop distance + leverage. For example, two traders both use 10x: one opens a very small position, the other puts almost all funds in. Their risks are completely different levels. So don't just ask "is 10x high?" Also ask "how big is the actual position in this trade?"

## 3️⃣ Beginners Should Practice "Fixed Risk" First, Not "Amplifying Profits"

Assume an account has 1,000 USDT. You can first set a maximum loss of 10-20 USDT per trade, then calculate position size based on entry price, stop distance, leverage, and position size. This is more logical than "I have 1000U, so I open 500U this time." Through a fixed risk model, you can survive longer in the market.

## 4️⃣ Long and Short Are Not Just Guessing Up or Down

**Long**: Don't just open long because "it has dropped a lot and should rebound." More reasonable logic includes: reclaiming a key level, support appearing after pullback, trend strengthening again, meeting preset bullish conditions.

**Short**: Don't just open short because "it has risen a lot and cannot continue." More reasonable logic includes: failure to break resistance, breaking key support, weakening rebound, meeting preset bearish conditions.

The real danger is not being wrong once, but placing orders continuously without logic and relying only on feelings.

## 5️⃣ First Determine "When Not to Trade"

Many people only look for where to open, but a more mature mindset also knows when not to trade. For example: price has already surged quickly, stop distance is too far, risk-reward ratio is inappropriate, volatility is suddenly abnormal, direction is unclear, or emotional instability. Sometimes, not opening a position is itself a trading decision.

## 6️⃣ Market Order Is Not a Universal Choice

**Market Order**: Suitable when immediate execution is needed. Fast and simple, but slippage may occur during violent fluctuations.

**Limit Order**: Suitable when there is a clear entry zone. For example, if you only want to go long after BTC returns to a certain level, you can place a Limit Order in advance. Easier to control entry price, no need to chase. Downside: if price doesn't reach, order won't execute.

## 7️⃣ After Opening, Watch These 4 Numbers at Least

1. Average Entry Price: your actual holding cost.
2. Current Price: where the market is now.
3. Stop Loss Level: where you plan to exit if wrong.
4. Liquidation Price: where the position may be forcibly closed if margin is insufficient.

Many only look at "how much I'm making now?" but ignore how far from stop loss or liquidation. This is a dangerous habit.

## 8️⃣ Profitable Trades Also Need Management

Risk management is not only needed during losses. A profitable trade also needs a plan. Consider partial profit-taking, trailing stop, holding part of position, closing all at target. Unplanned profits can turn back into losses.

## 9️⃣ Trailing Stop Needs Rules

Trailing stop itself is fine. The problem is: price nears stop → move stop back → nears again → move further back. This is usually not risk management, but "unwilling to accept loss." A more reasonable trailing stop should occur after price moves favorably, to protect profits.

## 🔟 Don't Automatically Increase Position After Consecutive Losses

Assume 3 consecutive losses. Many think "increase size next time to recover." This easily turns into revenge trading. Better approach: pause trading, check for repeated mistakes, reduce size, wait for qualified opportunities. After losses, focus on restoring judgment quality, not immediate profit recovery.

## 1️⃣1️⃣ Don't Use Adding to Losing Position as Stop Loss Substitute

Common scenario: position loses → add a bit → lose more → add more → position grows. Adding itself isn't wrong if designed beforehand. You should know: max adds, size per add, total risk limit, final exit point. If just because "already losing and don't want to leave," it's not a complete strategy.

## 1️⃣2️⃣ Trading Frequency Is Also Part of Risk

Some trade 10, 20 times a day. Problem isn't just fees, but judgment fatigue, emotional swings, overtrading, chasing, and loose rules. More trades don't mean more opportunities. Often, doing less and filtering better is wiser.

## 1️⃣4️⃣ Beginners Don't Need Complex Strategies First

Start with 3 things:
1. Fixed risk per trade: bear only small portion of account.
2. Exit rule for every trade: no position without stop loss plan.
3. Post-trade review: record why enter, why exit, what went wrong, if plan violated. Execution matters more than piling indicators early on.

## 1️⃣5️⃣ How to Keep Trading Records

Record for each trade: Pair, Direction (Long/Short), Entry Reason, Entry Price, Stop Loss, First TP, Final Target, Leverage, Position Size, Max Loss, Actual Result, Review. Over time, you'll spot your most common mistakes.
